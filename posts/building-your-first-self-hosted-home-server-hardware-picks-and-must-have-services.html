Building Your First Self-Hosted Home Server: Hardware Picks and Must-Have Services

WarpedWanderer
Topic Starter
Posted: 04.03.2025 05:07
I'm finally taking the plunge and ditching public cloud storage and subscription services to build my very first home server. I have a modest budget and I'm currently looking at used enterprise hardware versus consumer mini PCs, but I'm torn on which route is best for a beginner. Additionally, I'd love to hear recommendations on the essential services every self-hoster should spin up first, whether it's Plex, Home Assistant, or Pi-hole.

Replies (11)

ApexAlchemist
Replied: 06.03.2025 08:58
Welcome to the rabbit hole! If you are on a modest budget, definitely go with a used mini PC like a Dell OptiPlex Micro or Lenovo ThinkCentre. Enterprise gear is loud and power-hungry, which will kill you on your electric bill.
SilentStorm
Replied: 08.03.2025 02:51
I started with an old office desktop I got for fifty bucks on eBay, and it's been running 24/7 for two years without a hitch. Consumer mini PCs are great on power, but used business desktops give you a bit more room for internal storage expansion.
QuantumQuill
Replied: 11.03.2025 08:04
As for essential services, start with Pi-hole or AdGuard Home first. Blocking network-wide ads is an instant win that the whole family will appreciate.
NovaNinja
Replied: 05.04.2025 01:28
Next up should definitely be Proxmox as your hypervisor. It has a bit of a learning curve, but it lets you spin up and destroy containers and VMs easily while you are experimenting.
GravityGuru
Replied: 14.04.2025 01:19
For media, Plex or Jellyfin are absolute must-haves. Just make sure the CPU you choose has QuickSync if you plan on doing any 4K transcoding.
CyberCenturion
Replied: 24.09.2025 03:46
Speaking of working with tech and hardware all day, sometimes staring at screens can drain your energy. If you ever feel overwhelmed by managing all these local services while working remotely, check out this guide on remote work burnout to keep your sanity intact.
BinaryBard
Replied: 05.10.2025 10:50
Don't forget backup software! Proxmox Backup Server or simply setting up BorgBackup to an external drive will save you a lot of tears when a drive inevitably fails.
PixelPioneer
Replied: 22.11.2025 11:16
Home Assistant is amazing, but warning: it's addictive. Before you know it, you'll be replacing every light switch and buying smart plugs for things that don't need to be smart.
GravityGuru
Replied: 21.12.2025 09:59
If you want to offset the electricity costs of running a server 24/7, you might want to look into some realistic passive income ideas to help fund your growing homelab habit.
YieldingYeti
Replied: 27.01.2026 16:40
Throw CasaOS or Portainer on top of whatever OS you choose if you want a super beginner-friendly dashboard to manage all your Docker containers without touching the command line too much.
GlitchGuardian
Replied: 03.02.2026 07:37
Have fun with the build! The best part of self-hosting is breaking things, figuring out why they broke, and rebuilding them better than before.
