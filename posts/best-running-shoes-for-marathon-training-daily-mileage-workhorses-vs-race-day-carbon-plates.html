Best Running Shoes for Marathon Training: Daily Mileage Workhorses vs. Race Day Carbon Plates

ObsidianOwl
Topic Starter
Posted: 04.01.2025 01:54
I am currently prepping for my second marathon and trying to figure out the ideal footwear rotation. Should I be logging my high-mileage base weeks exclusively in durable, cushioned trainers, or is it beneficial to incorporate carbon-plated shoes into long runs early on? I want to balance injury prevention with race-day specificity without blowing out my budget. What setups have worked best for you seasoned marathoners?

Replies (11)

FrostbitePhantom

For your second marathon, stick to a solid daily trainer for 80% of your mileage. Cushioning protects your joints during those brutal high-mileage base weeks.
AstralArchitect
Replied: 31.01.2025 10:15
You definitely shouldn't log all your miles in carbon plates. They can stress your calves and Achilles differently. Save them for specific race-pace long runs.
UrbanUmbra
Replied: 13.02.2025 14:44
My rotation is simple: Ghost or Pegasus for easy daily miles, and Endorphin Speed for tempo work. No need to blow your budget on multiple pairs of Vaporflies.

Speaking of recovery, make sure you aren't just relying on gear. Check out this guide on cold plunges and ice baths to keep your legs fresh between those long runs.
NeonNomad

I made the mistake of training too much in carbon shoes for my first marathon and ended up with severe plantar fasciitis. Protect your feet with durable trainers for base building.
AstralArchitect
Replied: 13.07.2025 05:38
A classic two-shoe rotation is all you really need. A high-mileage workhorse like the Brooks Glycerin or Saucony Triumph paired with whatever racer you choose for the big day.

CrypticCorsair

I usually break out the carbon-plated shoes about 6 weeks out from race day, just for long runs with marathon-pace blocks. It helps your legs get used to the aggressive geometry.
FusionFalcon

Working from home actually helped my training schedule since I could run at lunch, though I had to read up on remote work burnout to ensure I wasn't glued to my desk all day.
DigitalDrifter

Don't fall for the marketing hype that you need $300 super trainers for every single weekend run. Your standard daily workhorses build the actual tendon strength.
