Digital Nomad Tax Residency: How Do You Manage Taxes and Banking Legally While Traveling?

SilentStorm
Topic Starter
Posted: 01.01.2025 09:49
I've been living the nomad lifestyle for about six months now, moving between Southeast Asia and Europe, but the tax and banking situation is starting to give me a major headache. How do you all handle establishing tax residency without a permanent home, and which international banks are actually friendly to nomads without a fixed residential address? I want to make sure I'm doing everything completely above board to avoid any nasty surprises down the road.

The tax residency issue is definitely the hardest part of this lifestyle. Generally, you want to maintain a tax home somewhere unless you qualify for something like the US Foreign Earned Income Exclusion. Otherwise, you might accidentally become a tax resident nowhere or everywhere at once!

For banking, Wise and Revolut have been absolute lifesavers for me. They let you hold multiple currencies and don't freak out as long as you can verify your identity with a passport. Just make sure you update your mailing address to a trusted friend or family member's house back home.

Never lie to banks about your residency. If they catch you using a VPN or a fake address, they will freeze your account instantly. Use a mail forwarding service that provides a real street address instead of a P.O. Box.

Setting up an LLC in Wyoming or Delaware is a common route for US citizens. It keeps your business legal and separate, but you still have to figure out your personal state tax obligations based on your last known domicile.

As a permanent traveler, the 183-day rule is your best friend and your worst enemy. You have to track every single day you spend in a country on a spreadsheet. I use a dedicated travel app just to keep logs for border control and tax authorities.
